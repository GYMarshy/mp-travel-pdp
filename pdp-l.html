Description

Meet the Ocarro 2 — the ultimate co-pilot for navigating your parenting journey. Building on our original award-winning model, we've elevated performance and design to deliver a next-generation pushchair with optimum functionality, flexibility and style.

Upgraded wheels and brakes for greater off-road efficiency, plush fabrics for superior cocoon-like comfort, and a compact fold-and-carry for effortless collapse and storage. An overnight-sleep-approved carrycot and stand are included for safe sleeping anytime, anywhere.

Combining the convenience you need and the style you crave with the adaptability to nurture your little one from birth to 4 years*, Ocarro 2 raises the 'all-rounder' bar to a whole new level.

*Up to 22 kg.

Prototype framework 18 design hypotheses to validate ▾

This prototype is a framework for testing, not a finished page. Each item below is a deliberate design choice with a hypothesis we'd validate post-launch via A/B testing or qualitative research.

  1. T1. Mixed-weight tagline below the H1 — M&P brand signature ("regular line / bold line"). Replaces the previous all-caps Black+Blum-style editorial headline which was off-brand. Hypothesis: an editorial-voice tagline in brand-true type lifts above-the-fold engagement vs no tagline; mixed-weight reads more "designed" than single-weight at the same length.
  2. T2. Brand-first title hierarchy — brand, then product, then bundle as subtitle. Hypothesis: separating the bundle name from the H1 reduces cognitive load on PDP landing.
  3. T3. Why-buy-me as a clean four-line key-features list. Hypothesis: short benefit lines in M&P type are more scannable than icon badges or paragraph copy. Test against image-baked-in and against text bullets only.
  4. T4. "Save £X compared to buying separately" inline copy. Hypothesis: clearer than "Worth £X · Save £Y" tooltip (which workshop participants misread as a sale price), especially on mobile where the tooltip is currently broken.
  5. T5. Bundle picker uses base+delta pattern. Hypothesis: showing what each option adds (vs duplicating the full list per tile) makes the differences instantly readable.
  6. T6. Car seat as sub-selector inside the bundle option (Ken V2 model mock). Hypothesis: separating "which car seat" from "which bundle" eliminates the duplicate-named tiles problem.
  7. T7. What's-included as a collapsible details element. Hypothesis: saves vertical space while keeping the list one click away.
  8. T8. "Help me choose" wizard as a button-launched modal. Hypothesis: addresses the validation-need without taking PDP real-estate. Track usage rate and downstream conversion.
  9. T9. Pram-level (not bundle-level) "Available to view in store". Hypothesis: pram-level avoids the false negative where customers don't visit because their specific car seat colour isn't on display.
  10. T10. Compatibility checker as embedded list, not a searcher. Hypothesis: shorter to answer "is mine compatible?" by scanning a list of common seats than by drilling through a make/model search.
  11. T11. Compare pushchairs as a drawer launched from a button, not a page section. Includes a preview row of product thumbs above the launcher as a teaser. Hypothesis: customers in comparison mode self-select; surfacing as opt-in keeps the PDP focused without losing the feature.
  12. T12. Real trust badges only at pushchair level (5 Year Guarantee · Quality Tested in the UK · Tested to BS EN 1888-2:2018 · Price Match Promise). i-Size / R129 only surfaces in car seat content. Hypothesis: verifiable claims build more confidence than vague endorsements; nothing fabricated, nothing misapplied.
  13. T13. Demo videos as a uniform 4-tile row with captions beneath, not a hero collage. Hypothesis: equal-weight thumbnails (iCandy pattern) drive more engagement than one large hero + smaller tiles with overlaid captions.
  14. T14. UGC as three large tiles with named handles, not a grid of placeholders. Hypothesis: fewer, larger, real-feeling photos read as authentic social proof; placeholder grids feel fake. Requires real influencer content (marketing brief).
  15. T15. Accessories upsell with live running total. Hypothesis: showing the impact of each tick on the total is a clearer cross-sell than a static list.
  16. T16. Remove quantity stepper. Hypothesis: very few customers buy more than one pushchair; the stepper adds clutter without value.
  17. T17. Live-video demo as a major editorial block below "What's in the box" — image-led, animated "live now" pulse, named experts, clear "Start a video call" CTA. Reinforced by smaller live-expert pings at three friction points (buybox, compat fallback, compare section). M&P's unfair advantage as a multi-channel retailer (per Chris in the transcript). Hypothesis: a prominent live-video block converts dramatically more demo sessions than the passive floating chat-bot today, especially at high-consideration price points.
  18. T18. Information baked into hero imagery (age range, key benefits, certifications). Hypothesis: people scroll image galleries even when they skip text. Requires marketing/photography brief for new lifestyle shots with embedded copy.

Open items, not in this prototype: title-length business decision (H1 / H2 mix and SEO impact); bundles V2 data model (single Ocarro PDP with colour + bundle + car seat as variants — IT dependency, Q3/Q4); review surfacing at pram-level vs bundle-level; image specs in marketing brief for new lifestyle / info-baked imagery; real pushchair safety certifications (BS EN 1888-2:2018 used as placeholder — needs sign-off from product team).
