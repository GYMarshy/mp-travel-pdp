Hypotheses to validate

This prototype is a framework for testing, not a finished page. Each marked element below is a deliberate design choice with a hypothesis we'd validate post-launch via A/B testing or qualitative research. Toggle the pink button top-right to see markers inline.

  1. T1. Editorial headline above the H1 (Black+Blum pattern). Hypothesis: an advertising-voice line lifts above-the-fold engagement vs utilitarian title alone.
  2. T2. Brand-first title hierarchy — brand, then product, then bundle as subtitle. Hypothesis: separating the bundle name from the H1 reduces cognitive load on PDP landing.
  3. T3. Why-buy-me as visual icon badges, not text bullets. Hypothesis: visual badges are more scannable and recalled higher than text. Test against text bullets and baked-into-imagery variants.
  4. T4. "£X less than buying everything separately" inline copy. Hypothesis: clearer than "Worth £X · Save £Y" tooltip, especially on mobile where the tooltip is currently broken.
  5. T5. Bundle picker uses base+delta pattern. Hypothesis: showing what each option adds (vs duplicating the full list per tile) makes the differences instantly readable.
  6. T6. Car seat as sub-selector inside the bundle option (Ken V2 model mock). Hypothesis: separating "which car seat" from "which bundle" eliminates the duplicate-named tiles problem.
  7. T7. What's-included as a collapsible details element. Hypothesis: saves vertical space while keeping the list one click away.
  8. T8. "Help me choose" wizard as a button-launched modal. Hypothesis: addresses the validation-need without taking PDP real-estate. Track usage rate and downstream conversion.
  9. T9. Pram-level (not bundle-level) "Available to view in store". Hypothesis: pram-level avoids the false negative where customers don't visit because their specific car seat colour isn't on display.
  10. T10. Compatibility checker as embedded list, not a searcher. Hypothesis: shorter to answer "is mine compatible?" by scanning a list of common seats than by drilling through a make/model search.
  11. T11. Compare pushchairs as a drawer launched from a button, not a page section. Hypothesis: customers in comparison mode self-select; surfacing as opt-in keeps the PDP focused without losing the feature.
  12. T12. Real trust badges only at pushchair level (5 Year Guarantee · Quality Tested in the UK · Born & Nurtured in the UK). i-Size / R129 only surfaces in the car seat content (bundle "Adds:", car seat features, compatibility list) — it's a car seat standard, not a pushchair one. Hypothesis: verifiable claims build more confidence than vague endorsements; nothing fabricated, nothing misapplied.
  13. T13. One hero proof video + two thumbs + "watch more" link. Hypothesis: tucked-away pattern (iCandy) drives more engagement than a video library on the page.
  14. T14. UGC as three large tiles with named handles, not a grid of placeholders. Hypothesis: fewer, larger, real-feeling photos read as authentic social proof; placeholder grids feel fake.
  15. T15. Accessories upsell with live running total. Hypothesis: showing the impact of each tick on the total is a clearer cross-sell than a static list.
  16. T16. Remove quantity stepper. Hypothesis: very few customers buy more than one pushchair; the stepper adds clutter without value.
  17. T17. Promote Go-In-Store live agent as the fallback CTA on the compatibility checker. Hypothesis: contextual placement at the moment of friction drives more conversation starts than the floating chat-bot today.
  18. T18. Information baked into hero imagery (age range, certifications). Hypothesis: people scroll image galleries even when they skip text. Marketing/photography brief required.

Open items not in this prototype: title-length business decision (H1 / H2 mix and SEO impact); bundles V2 data model (single Ocarro PDP with colour + bundle + car seat as variants, IT dependency); review surfacing at pram-level vs bundle-level; image specs in marketing brief for new lifestyle/info-baked imagery.
